# SohoArchTimes blurry-image investigation and partial fix — 2026-05-26

## a) Root cause of the blur

The current public site rendered images from `cdn*.telesco.pe` URLs. Those URLs are scraped from the public `https://t.me/s/SohoArchTimes` widget; they are the **preview JPEGs Telegram serves to the web widget**, not the original upload. Concretely:

- For slide 209 / 335 (mid 621, "Shenzhen Science & Technology Museum"), the rendered image was `cdn4.telesco.pe/file/T5Se...jpg` at **800 × 574 px, ~50 KB**.
- The same image, as it exists on the original publisher (`images.adsttc.com/.../large_jpg/shenzhen-...-architects_2.jpg`), is **substantially larger, ~214 KB**, with markedly more detail.
- The previous `image_policy: telegram_faithful_v2` was deliberately conservative: it forbade rendering anything other than telesco.pe URLs, on the principle that "faithful to Telegram" means "exact bytes of the channel". The cost of that policy was visible softness on large displays.

So the user observation was correct, and the previous build's diagnostic that the site is "Telegram-faithful but blurry" was a description, not a defence.

## b) Can it be fixed fully or partially?

**Partially, and only where we can prove it.**

Per-post proof tiering on the 69 currently-rendered posts:

| Tier | Definition | Count | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Proven high-res | A `publish_results_*.json` entry links the publisher payload file to the Telegram `message_id`, the payload's `image_urls` survived locally, AND `len(payload.image_urls) == len(telegram_preview_images)` | **21 posts / 105 slides** | Render publisher's original URLs (e.g. `images.adsttc.com/.../large_jpg/...`); keep the matching telesco.pe URL as a per-slide fallback. |
| B — Preview only | No publish_results proof binding the payload to the mid, OR payload missing locally | **47 posts / 227 slides** | Keep `cdn*.telesco.pe` preview (current behaviour). |
| C — Count mismatch | publish_results proof exists, but the upload count diverges from the Telegram preview count (e.g. one of the 5 uploads failed silently) | **1 post / 3 slides** (mid 746 "Quzhou Sports Park") | Refuse the swap. Keep telesco.pe preview. We cannot prove ordering or identity of the 3 published images vs. the 5 in the payload. |

So **21 of 69 posts (~30 %)** are fixed with strong proof — including mid 621, the case the user cited. The other 48 stay at preview resolution because there is no honest way to know whether the locally-stored "source images" are the ones that were actually uploaded:

- For 41 of those 48, the `source_image_urls` come from a **2026-05-16 archive-recovery pass** (`archive_recovery_report.md`) that walked publisher gallery pages and *kept the first 5*. That is a heuristic reconstruction, not proof. Using those URLs is precisely the past failure mode where the site showed images not in Telegram.
- For 6 posts, even the heuristic source set doesn't match preview count (e.g. mid 911 Orange County Museum of Art has no recovered URLs; mid 431 Museum of the Future has 5 recovered but the actual Telegram post has only 2 photos).
- For mid 746 (Quzhou Sports Park), we have publish_results proof — but Telegram only has 3 photos for a 5-photo payload, so we can't determine which 3 were the survivors.

## e) Remaining risks

1. **ArchDaily / publisher CDN outages.** If `images.adsttc.com` (or another publisher host) becomes unreachable, the 21 proven posts would lose their primary image. Mitigation: every `proven_high_res` slide ships with its matching `url_fallback` pointing at the original `cdn*.telesco.pe` preview, and `app.js` now retries the fallback automatically on load failure. Worst case is the slide silently degrades back to the previous (preview) experience, not a black frame.
2. **Telegram re-encoding vs publisher original.** The publisher's `_normalize_to_jpeg` pre-scales images >2560 px down to 2560 and re-encodes at JPEG q=88 before upload, then Telegram further re-encodes for previews. The original publisher URL is *visually identical content* but at higher resolution than what is stored inside Telegram. This is honest about content but **not bit-identical** to the channel. If the strict definition of "Telegram-faithful" is "exact bytes from the channel", that is a policy relaxation. The previous `image_policy: telegram_faithful_v2` enforced bit-faithfulness; the new `telegram_faithful_v3_proven_high_res` enforces only content-faithfulness for posts with strong publication proof.
3. **48 preview-only posts remain soft.** Roughly 70 % of currently-visible posts are still rendered at ~800 px. We cannot fix them without changing the publication pipeline (see point 5).
4. **Single-post regression risk for 746 (Quzhou Sports Park).** Telegram has 3 photos; payload had 5. We refused the swap and kept the preview. The site is no worse than before. Re-publishing the post would let us collect a clean proof.
5. **Future fix requires pipeline change.** The honest way to make *every* post sharp is to have `publish_album.py` persist, at publish time, a verified mapping `message_id → list[uploaded_image_url]` (e.g. write it back into `published_objects.json` next to `telegram_full_image_urls`). After that, every new post automatically becomes Tier A. Backfilling existing soft posts would still require either (a) re-publishing those posts, or (b) accepting that the "first-5 from gallery" heuristic is the closest available match and using it explicitly with a different UI signal. Either is a policy call, not a code change I should make unilaterally.

## Honest summary

The site looked blurry because it was deliberately serving Telegram's downsized preview JPEGs, and that policy was conservative for good reason — we had previously shown images that were not in the channel. The fix here rebuilds a strong, evidence-based bridge from `publish_results_*.json` to each Telegram message and only upgrades resolution where the proof holds. 21 of 69 visible posts (≈30 %) are now sharp at their actual publisher resolution; the other 48 stay soft and there is no honest way to upgrade them without either re-publishing them or relaxing the "must match what's in the channel" rule. The pipeline change in point 5 above is the only way to make this 100 %.
